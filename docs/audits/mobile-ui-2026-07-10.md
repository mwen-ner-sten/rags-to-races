# Mobile UI audit — 2026-07-10

## Scope

- Combined UX, visual-layout, responsive, stacking, and accessibility-risk audit.
- Live visual capture in Chrome mobile emulation, primarily at 400×1176, with a 390×844 browser-suite cross-check.
- States reviewed: onboarding, early/empty, jumpstarted/populated, tutorial result, and late-game Team/Owner/Track fixtures.
- Screens reviewed: all five primary destinations; all eight Workshop sections; all six Help sections; Legacy, Scrap Reset, Trophies, Team, Owner, and Track progression; More, Activity, Settings, and developer tools.
- Evidence is stored beside this report. Contact sheets summarize the complete capture set; the numbered PNGs retain full-resolution evidence.

## Overall verdict

The mobile shell is fundamentally sound: page-level horizontal overflow is controlled, the fixed bottom navigation remains reachable, primary gameplay screens reflow into a usable single column, and the Midnight Circuit visual language remains coherent. The experience is not yet consistently polished across the whole application, however. One screen is visibly broken on mobile (Garage Philosophy), several sections rely on nested/native scrollbars, and multiple locked or empty surfaces leave most of the viewport unused without a useful next action. Dense late-game screens also let the fixed nav cover content during scrolling.

## Highest-impact findings

### P0 — Garage Philosophy is not mobile-responsive

Evidence: `10-workshop-philosophy.png`.

The three philosophy trees remain side-by-side at phone width. Labels, descriptions, prices, and prerequisite lines overlap heavily. Controls become difficult to read and risky to tap. This is the only captured screen that is functionally compromised by its layout.

Recommendation: switch to one philosophy column at mobile widths, or use a segmented selector/accordion that shows one tree at a time. Preserve the three-column comparison only at tablet/desktop widths.

### P1 — Workshop navigation is visually heavy and hides destinations

Evidence: `05` through `12`.

The eight-section tab row overflows horizontally and exposes a desktop-style native scrollbar. Later destinations such as Philosophy, Skills, and Facilities are initially hidden. The scrollbar consumes vertical space and makes a core navigation control look like a data viewport.

Recommendation: use the same compact dropdown pattern as Help, or a two-row mobile section grid. If horizontal tabs remain, hide the native scrollbar, add edge fades, and auto-scroll the active tab into view.

### P1 — Nested scrolling is widespread

Evidence: Scrap Reset milestones (`14`), Help Systems (`18`), Help Progression (`20`), and the Junkyard inventory.

Several screens place independently scrollable panels inside a page that also scrolls. On a phone this creates scroll handoff ambiguity, multiple visible scrollbar thumbs, and uncertainty about whether more content exists in the page or the card.

Recommendation: default to document scrolling on mobile. Keep internal scroll regions only when a panel must remain bounded, and give those regions a clear title, count, and bottom affordance.

### P1 — Locked and empty states waste most of the viewport

Evidence: Workshop Add-ons (`07`), Dealer (`08`), and Activity (`23`).

These screens explain why content is unavailable, but then leave a large empty field. They do not provide a direct route to the prerequisite or a useful secondary action.

Recommendation: include a compact prerequisite card with progress and a CTA such as “Open Facilities,” “Earn Rep in Race,” or “Start scavenging.” Activity should offer a direct “Go to Junkyard” action when empty.

### P1 — Fixed mobile navigation obscures dense late-game content

Evidence: Team (`27`), Owner (`28`), and Track (`29`).

The fixed bar remains usable, but it sits over cards and purchase buttons while the user scrolls. Full-page evidence shows controls disappearing underneath the bar before reappearing below it.

Recommendation: maintain bottom padding equal to nav height plus safe-area inset on every mobile content container, and add `scroll-margin-bottom` to focusable controls so keyboard or programmatic focus never lands behind the bar.

### P2 — Header density is high

Evidence: nearly every gameplay screen.

Logo, theme identity, currencies, vehicle identity, performance, fatigue, and momentum compete in the first 140–160px. The hierarchy survives, but labels and numbers become small and scan-heavy.

Recommendation: keep primary currency/Rep visible, but collapse secondary run stats into a single tappable status row on phones. Consider abbreviating or moving the vehicle detail below the header.

### P2 — Horizontal carousels expose native scrollbars

Evidence: Junkyard (`02`) and Workshop (`05`–`12`).

Location cards communicate continuation through a clipped next card, which is good, but the thick native scrollbar looks unfinished and consumes space.

Recommendation: preserve the clipped-card cue, hide the native scrollbar, add scroll snapping, and use a subtle edge gradient or pagination count.

### P2 — Several touch targets are below 44×44px

Evidence: Settings (`24`) and measurements from the live page.

Examples include 28px Save/Load controls, 28–30px import/export controls, 36px Scavenge, small Workshop tabs, and footer links. The primary bottom nav is appropriately sized.

Recommendation: use 44px minimum hit areas for frequently used controls, even when the visible button remains visually compact.

### P2 — Onboarding/tutorial overlays need stronger modal semantics

Evidence: onboarding (`01`) and tutorial result (`26`).

The overlays are visually clear and the corrected tutorial halo now stacks above mobile navigation. Risks remain: underlying navigation is still visible, screenshot evidence cannot confirm focus trapping, and the markup does not visually communicate a native dialog boundary. The mobile suite also found a duplicate hidden repair message before the visible tutorial copy, which made one selector choose hidden text.

Recommendation: apply `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"`, a labelled title, focus entry/return, and inert underlying content. Remove duplicate hidden instructional copy from the accessibility tree when a tutorial modal supplies the same message.

### P3 — Footer duplicates mobile navigation

Evidence: tutorial and long-content screenshots.

“Circuit” and “Settings” remain in the footer immediately above the fixed mobile nav. This adds visual noise and can sit behind tutorial cards.

Recommendation: hide footer navigation controls on mobile; retain only legal/version text if needed.

## Confirmed strengths

- No page-level horizontal overflow was detected across the primary mobile screens or the maxed-state browser suite.
- The bottom navigation has clear active states, useful labels, and stable reachability.
- The More tray is compact, legible, and anchored correctly above the nav.
- Garage, Race preparation, Facilities, Skills, Settings, and simulator layouts form coherent single-column mobile pages.
- Information cards use consistent borders, spacing, headings, and semantic color.
- Race inputs reflow into a readable two-column grid instead of overflowing.
- Help uses a mobile-specific section selector rather than squeezing six tabs into one row.
- Settings uses a readable two-column theme selector and clearly separates save, import/export, and tutorial controls.
- The semantic color palette passed the existing contrast regression check; screenshots alone do not prove contrast for every theme/state.

## Accessibility risks and verification gaps

- Screenshots confirm responsive reflow and visible focus targets, but cannot establish full keyboard order, screen-reader announcements, focus trapping, or reduced-motion behavior.
- The 390px suite exercised keyboard Scavenge, primary navigation, theme overflow, and serious structural accessibility checks. It passed 41 of 42 tests.
- The single failing test displayed the correct tutorial result card; the failure came from a hidden duplicate repair message being matched before the visible card. This is a test robustness issue and a possible accessibility-tree duplication risk, not a visible tutorial failure.
- Several controls are below WCAG’s recommended 44×44 CSS-pixel target size.
- Emoji-only imagery is generally paired with text in navigation, but decorative emoji inside headings/cards should be checked for redundant screen-reader output.
- Disabled text is intentionally dim and may fall below normal contrast requirements; disabled controls are exempt from some contrast criteria but still need to remain understandable.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Rebuild Garage Philosophy for one-column mobile presentation.
2. Replace Workshop’s native horizontal scrollbar navigation.
3. Remove or minimize nested scrollers on mobile.
4. Add actionable locked/empty states for Add-ons, Dealer, and Activity.
5. Guarantee bottom-nav clearance and focus scroll margins across every screen.
6. Increase hit areas for compact buttons and tabs.
7. Simplify the mobile header and hide duplicate footer navigation.
8. Harden dialog semantics and remove duplicate hidden tutorial instructions.
